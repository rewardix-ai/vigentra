How Vigentra reads

05The signal chain

On screen
Six numbered stages, each showing the real data at that step, two YES/NO decisions, and outcomes with real refused plates: Not read, No plate saved. A square follows one car; the event log says what each stage does as it arrives. Red squares take the NO lines.
Say
Here is the whole method on one screen, following that real car. BEAT The camera gives us video. We find the car and track it, so it keeps one identity. Inside the car, and only inside it, we look for the plate. We keep its best frames and combine them. BEAT Then the first decision: are there enough pixels? If not, it is not read at all. If yes, we read the plate. BEAT Then the second decision: do the readings agree, and is it a valid Indian format? If not, no plate is saved. PAUSE Only when both answers are yes do we have a confirmed plate: the registration, the time and the camera. Never the video. The red squares you see are plates that took a NO line: that is the system declining to guess.
Land this
The steps, and that Vigentra refuses rather than guesses.

The system

08The high-level design

On screen
A live schematic: cameras, edge in the site, regional per district, central for the state, the console; a red dashed line between edge and regional marks where only metadata crosses. Squares carry video into the edge and text beyond it.
Say
Now zoom out to the state. The design has four tiers. BEAT Cameras stay with their departments, on their own video systems. The edge, inside the department's site, reads the video where it is recorded: plates, vehicle types, helmets and footage quality. Each district has a regional tier that speaks the local systems' dialects and holds records if the network to the centre drops. Central keeps the records and decides who may see what. PAUSE The red line is the point of the design: only metadata crosses it, never video. And each tier grows on its own: more GPUs, more districts or more central replicas, without touching the others.
Land this
Why it scales: video stays local, text travels.

11What it needs

On screen
Two big numbers: about 160 Gbps if video went central, about 1 Gbps with Vigentra; then the requirements table, tier by tier: edge GPUs, regional, central, storage, integrations, people, cost.
Say
And here is what it takes, sized honestly. If every camera's video were sent to one data centre, you would need about a hundred and sixty gigabits a second of network, and storage for about one point seven petabytes of video every day. Vigentra sends only metadata: about one gigabit a second for the whole state. BEAT What it does need: about three thousand GPU nodes at the edge, in the departments' own sites, with plate reading only on cameras whose view allows it. Two nodes per district. Two central sites, one a disaster-recovery copy. Metadata kept hot for thirty days, warm for a year, cold for seven, with the audit log kept in full. Access to each department's video system and the state network. People in every district to confirm alerts, and a small team labelling footage. The edge GPUs are the one large cost, ninety to a hundred and twenty crore over the phases; the software has no licence fees.
Land this
It is feasible, and the cost is in one known place.

12What makes Vigentra different

On screen
Six panels: nothing new at the camera (Model 1 + Model 3); a wrong plate is worse than a missed one (the zero-false rule); built for Indian roads (40 state codes, 39 Gujarat RTOs); private by design (retention enforced on read); trained on Gujarat's own footage (4,891 plate boxes, 2,160 vehicles, 1,317 riders labelled by people); the department stays in control (grant, deny, revoke).
Say
So, to close: what makes Vigentra different? BEAT Nothing new at the camera: it federates what each department already runs. BEAT A wrong plate counts for more than a missed one: we rejected a model that read two more plates because it added one wrong plate. BEAT It is built for Indian roads: forty state codes, thirty-nine Gujarat RTOs, Indian vehicle classes and MV Act sections. BEAT It is private by design: no central video, and plate records expire on a clock checked on every read. BEAT It is trained on Gujarat's own footage, labelled by people: almost five thousand plate boxes, over two thousand vehicles and over thirteen hundred riders. BEAT And every department stays in control of its own video.
