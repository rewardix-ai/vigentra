Under the hood

19What Vigentra is built with

On screen
Eight panels: capture (RTSP over TCP), vehicles (YOLO11s + ByteTrack), plates (YOLO11n inside each vehicle), reading (two CRNN readers + PaddleOCR), deciding (votes, plate rules, glyph check), central (FastAPI, PostgreSQL), console (Next.js, Leaflet, OpenStreetMap), delivery (Docker).
Say
Under the hood, it is all open source. Video comes in over RTSP, the protocol the cameras already speak. YOLO11 finds the vehicles and ByteTrack gives each one an identity, so a plate is decided once per vehicle. The plate detector looks only inside the vehicle. Two kinds of reader read it, because different readers make different mistakes. Then the vote, the Indian plate rules and the glyph check decide. The centre is FastAPI and PostgreSQL, with no video and no camera passwords. The console is Next.js on OpenStreetMap, and it all ships in Docker.
Land this
Every part is a deliberate, open choice.
Then
You move on.

20Why this, not that

On screen
A table of seven decisions: what Vigentra uses, instead of what, and why it is better.
Say
Why these choices? The AI runs next to the video, not in one centre: forty to two hundred and fifty times less network. A plate is read once per vehicle, from all its frames: the single-frame reader we started with got one plate from sixty-seven vehicles. Only what many frames agree on is saved: zero wrong. Two kinds of reader: sixteen plates instead of thirteen. ByteTrack, because fixed cameras need no camera-motion step. Watchlist matching priced by real OCR errors, so one wrong character still raises an alert. And video is brokered, so revoking access stops a stream within one segment.
Land this
Each choice is measured or reasoned, not fashion.
Then
You move on.

21What we tested and dropped

On screen
Six rows: confirming on 3 crops (a wrong plate on Delhi), 20 crops (no gain), two CRNNs backing each other (shared mistakes), frames enlarged to 1920 px (half the vehicles, slower), a plate box up to 0.9 of its vehicle (a hoarding read), one frame at a time (1 plate from 67 vehicles).
Say
We also measured what not to do. Confirming on three crops instead of four let a wrong plate through, so it is four. Twenty crops instead of twelve found nothing more. Our two CRNN readers share mistakes, so they cannot vouch for each other. Enlarging frames halved the vehicles found. A wide plate box let a hoarding be read. And reading one frame at a time found one plate in sixty-seven vehicles. Every change was replayed on the same clips before we kept it.
Land this
Every threshold has a measured reason.

Why Vigentra

25What makes Vigentra different

On screen
Six panels: nothing new at the camera (Model 1 + Model 3); a wrong plate is worse than a missed one (the zero-false rule); built for Indian roads (40 state codes, 39 Gujarat RTOs); private by design (retention enforced on read); trained on Gujarat's own footage (4,891 plate boxes, 2,160 vehicles, 1,317 riders labelled by people); the department stays in control (grant, deny, revoke).
Say
So, to close: what makes Vigentra different? Nothing new at the camera. A wrong plate counts for more than a missed one, so a model that adds one is not used. Built for Indian roads. Private by design: no central video, and plates expire on a clock checked on every read. Trained on our own footage, labelled by people. And every department stays in control of its video.
Land this
Vigentra's choices: existing cameras, no guessing, federated video, Indian plates, accountability, health.
