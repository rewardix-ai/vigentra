One platform over Gujarat's CCTV — without asking 26 departments to replace theirs

A federated camera registry and GIS, live analytics at the edge, watchlist alerts and cross-camera vehicle tracking, running today on the 30-camera government feed.

The problem

Many systems, no shared view, no shared memory

26
government departments, each with its own CCTV and VMS
~80,000
cameras to reach statewide, analog and IP, many vendors
~1,000 km
between the furthest sites, on links that are not uniform
  • Vendors, AMC periods and storage differ; retention runs from 7 days to 15 or more.
  • No department can see another's cameras, or check what a camera sees against the records that would make it actionable.
  • The brief: one searchable platform, analysed and alerting in real time, that grows toward 80,000 cameras without a redesign.

The constraint that shapes everything

Departments keep running their own systems. The first department to join gets value on day one, and the twenty-sixth changes nothing for the first.

Model chosen, and why

A hybrid: Model 1 foundation, Model 3 integration, Model 2 console

1

Registry & GIS

Adopted in full

Mandatory, and correct: nothing else works without knowing which cameras exist, where, and who owns them. Useful on day one with no analytics at all.

3

VMS federation

The integration

One adapter per vendor, normalising to one camera model. Vendor differences stop at the adapter; a department joins without touching anything downstream.

2

Unified viewing

As the surface

One console for operators — but it talks to the platform, never directly to 26 VMS, so a new vendor is never a change to the screen people use.

4

Central VMS

Rejected

Streaming 80,000 cameras centrally is ~160 Gbps and ~1.7 PB a day, and puts every camera credential in one place. Inference goes to the video instead.

Video is pulled on demand, per authorised session. Analytics run next to the cameras and send only metadata.

Analog and legacy

An analog camera is reached through the DVR or encoder that digitises it, which exposes RTSP or ONVIF like any IP source.

Outages stay local

One department down leaves the rest working; its cameras go offline with a stated reason, and every failed poll is audited.

Liveness follows the video

If the grid's web gateway refuses but its video server answers, cameras stay online — this recovered 72 of 180 lost minutes on 14 Sep.

Grammar, not guesswork

A character's position says letter or digit, so GJ05JV34S6 can only be GJ05JV3456. Impossible states and districts are dropped at the edge.

One vehicle, one reading

Emitted once per vehicle with the number of frames that agreed. Unconfirmed readings are shown as unconfirmed, never as fact.

Object detection

Person, car, motorcycle, bus, truck, auto-rickshaw, bicycle — with a frame-quality router that skips frames too degraded to mean anything.

Skipping plate search on vehicles too small to read gave the same 36 readings on the labelled Delhi clip in 586 s instead of 878 s.

Watchlist alerts and vehicle tracking

Known at the moment of the read; traced across cameras

!

Matched at ingest

  • Every plate reading is checked against the active watchlist as it arrives — stolen, wanted, missing, blacklisted.
  • Fuzzy on purpose: a known OCR confusion (B↔8, O↔0) costs 0.35, an unrelated character 1.0.
  • Alerts say whether the match was exact, and near matches are shown, not hidden.
→

The route, from the plate

  • Given a registration: every permitted sighting in time order, with distance, elapsed time and speed per leg, on the map.
  • Repeated reads at one camera collapse into one pass; impossible legs are flagged, never dropped.
  • Every trace needs a written reason, recorded against the account.

This is the hackathon-day test case: a designated vehicle's complete route and timestamped, location-wise movement history.

Cybersecurity, privacy and audit

Nobody sees more as cameras are added

1

Brokered, opaque video

Short-lived, watermarked sessions; every segment is re-authorised, so revoking a grant stops a playing stream.

2

Scope in three dimensions

Department, city and zone, checked per request, plus each camera's own access policy.

3

Least privilege

13 roles, 22 permissions. The account that ingests plates cannot read the watchlist back.

4

Withheld, not refused

Without plate permission an officer still gets the alert, with the plate withheld.

5

Retention on read

Plates keep a shorter clock, applied at read time — a failed purge cannot extend it.

6

A complete audit trail

Every sign-in, read, grant, refusal and machine-raised alert, each committed on its own.

Our perspective

What we believe — and what the grid taught us

Start from the people who use it

A control-room officer needs one screen; twenty-six departments should not have to change theirs to give it to them. Federation, not replacement, is what makes that possible on day one.

Never show more certainty than we have

An unconfirmed reading is shown as unconfirmed, a plate too small to read is marked unreadable instead of guessed, and every figure in this deck was measured or is labelled as an estimate.

Privacy is the design, not a feature

Footage is asked for, never ambient. Every look is brokered and audited — refusals included — because the thing that scales worst in surveillance is how many people can see everything.

The hard part was the plumbing

On the government feed the model was rarely the problem: refused sessions, dropped streams and a shared account were. The platform now keeps cameras online while video flows and restarts its own readers.

This is what we were able to build in the time we had.

With upgraded hardware, the same software can do much better.

Today

One laptop — an Apple M1 with 16 GB. Our readers share its single GPU and visit the 30 cameras in turn, a few minutes each.

With edge GPUs

About one T4-class card per 15 cameras (estimate): every camera read continuously, heavier models, more frames of every vehicle.

With cameras placed for ANPR

Plates large enough to confirm rather than guess — on today's wide junction views it is the optics, not the code, that set the ceiling.
