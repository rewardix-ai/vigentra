Video stays with the department

Only text, numbers and times travel to the centre. Video is streamed through the API only to an officer who passes every check (step 20).

Unsure means silent

A plate that is not confirmed is never sent (step 10), and a near match only ever raises a review alert, never a critical one.

Everything leaves a trace

Uploads, alerts, video sessions, refusals and dismissals all go into the audit log, which is never edited.

A

Cameras join the platform

  1. Each department keeps running its own system: the Sentinel grid, the Traffic Police VMS and the Municipal VMS. Vigentra does not replace them. A new camera starts as an installer's request, which the owning department approves in its own system.
  2. At start-up, or when someone presses Synchronise, an adapter for each system reads its camera list and turns it into one standard camera record.
  3. The records land in the camera registry. A health monitor re-checks every camera every 20 seconds, so the map shows which ones are online.

Code: central-api/app/adapters/, services/sync_service.py, services/health_monitor.py

B

The edge reads plates, next to the camera

  1. The edge worker asks the registry which cameras it may read, then opens each one's live video over RTSP, through an audited session. The video stays on this machine.
  2. Not every frame is worth the work. The sampler picks frames where a plate could be readable, and dark or broken frames are skipped.
  3. YOLO11s finds the vehicles, and ByteTrack follows each one from frame to frame, so one vehicle keeps one ID.
  4. The plate is found inside each vehicle and its crop is saved, up to 64 crops per vehicle.
  5. Nothing is decided while the vehicle is still in view. Its track closes once it has been unseen for 45 frames.
  6. The best 12 crops are straightened and sharpened, read by two or three readers, and the readings are combined letter by letter.
  7. The plate counts as confirmed only when the readers agree strongly enough and the crop shows real characters. Otherwise nothing is sent, because a wrong plate is worse than no plate.
  8. A confirmed plate is uploaded at once, with its time and camera. Only text and numbers travel.

Code: edge-worker/app/worker.py, app/grid.py, app/anpr_engine.py, anpr/pipeline.py

C

The central API checks and decides

  1. Every upload is checked: the camera must be known and commissioned, in the sender's scope, and the time must make sense. A row sent twice counts as a repeat and is not stored again.
  2. The detection is saved. If it carries a real-looking Indian plate, a plate sighting is saved too, for plate search and reports.
  3. The plate is compared with every active watchlist entry. The comparison allows for common misreads, such as B read as 8.
  4. A match creates an alert. An exact stolen or wanted plate is critical, any other exact match is high, and a near match is review.
  5. Each alert is written to the audit log. If a webhook is set up, a signed message also goes to a dispatch or SMS system.

Code: central-api/app/routers/detections.py, services/watchlist_service.py, services/plate_matching.py, services/alert_webhook.py

D

People see and act

  1. An officer signs in to the dashboard and sees only what their role and unit allow. The same sign-in opens the camera map, live wall, plate search and reports.
  2. The alerts page refreshes every 5 seconds and puts the most urgent alerts first.
  3. The officer compares the plate image with the alert. If it is the right vehicle, they acknowledge it and respond. If not, they dismiss it with a reason, which helps tune the matching.
  4. To watch the camera, the officer re-enters their password. The role, the camera's state, the unit or a grant, and the area are all checked. Any failure is refused and logged.
  5. If everything passes, a short watermarked session opens: 5 minutes live, or 15 minutes for a recording. The video comes from the department's system through the API, and the camera's real address never reaches the browser.

Code: dashboard/middleware.ts, app/api/vigentra/[...path]/route.ts, app/alerts/page.tsx, central-api/app/services/video_permissions.py, video_broker.py

How an account works

Every request is checked against three layers, in this order. An account gets through only if all three allow it.

1 · Role: what kind of actions

The role lists what the account may do, such as reading plates, acting on alerts or uploading detections. Plates are a separate permission, because a registration number is personal data.

2 · Scope: which cameras

Department, city and zone. A Zone 3 officer does not see Zone 1's cameras as their own, and a city admin sees only their city.

3 · Camera: is it available

For video, the camera must be commissioned, enabled by its owner and healthy. Another unit's camera, or any camera for a central account, needs a grant from the owning unit.

Signing in gives a token that the dashboard keeps in a cookie page scripts cannot read. Every page asks the central API through the dashboard's proxy, and the API checks the three layers on each request.

Presenting it: the chart in seven sign-ins

One order for the demo that walks the chart from top to bottom and shows every layer of access.

  1. traffic.installer adds a camera with the form or a CSV. The Traffic VMS validates it, and after the next metadata sync it appears on the map (module 2, the “New camera?” box).
  2. state.admin sees every camera in the state and its health, but watching one requires asking the owning unit. This shows that metadata travels while video stays with the department (modules 1, 3 and 4).
  3. traffic.operator adds the demo plate to the watchlist (module 7, step 14).
  4. No sign-in for this step: the edge, signed in as traffic.ai, reads the vehicle and uploads the plate (module 5, steps 4–11).
  5. traffic.operator sees the alert within 5 seconds, acknowledges it, traces the vehicle and opens the camera (module 7 and module 4, steps 17–21).
  6. traffic.zone3 asks to watch a Zone 1 camera, and traffic.operator approves it under Access requests (module 4).
  7. auditor opens the audit log and finds every step above, including the refusals (module 9).

Not drawn: the live wall's still frames come from a separate frame service and pass through the API like any other video.
